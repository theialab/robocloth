Overview

Abstract

We present RoboCloth, a dataset of 500 real cloth materials captured under flexible, calibrated camera and lighting configurations, where both are robotically controlled during acquisition. Each material is imaged in approximately 580 high-dynamic range frames spanning a wide view-light hemisphere; camera poses are refined via structure-from-motion and aligned to the robot frame, yielding per-material sparse surface geometry and structured per-point reflectance observations ready for neural material training. We demonstrate the value of this data through a two-stage neural reconstruction pipeline: Stage 1 trains a shared MLP decoder on our corpus to learn cloth material priors; Stage 2 then freezes the decoder and fits a dense 2048×2048 latent texture to each target material. Evaluating on held-out materials from our own dataset and on the independent UBO2014 benchmark, we show that a decoder trained on our captures outperforms decoders trained on existing measured reflectance datasets and analytic PBR fitting, transferring to unseen acquisition systems without modification. The dataset, calibration files, observation tensors, and train/test splits are released under CC BY 4.0 on the Hugging Face Hub.

Rendering note Each tile is a trained 2048×2048 latent texture, coloured by decoding every texel with the frozen decoder under a 45° light. Dropped on an object, it becomes the full material (relief, sheen and view dependence), which is path-traced on the piece you choose; every piece takes its own material. Each piece is pre-rendered in every material (3840 px, 1024 spp; 1920 px on phones and standard screens) and the combination is assembled live; the light a dressed piece throws onto the rest of the room is added as a smooth low-resolution correction.

Cloth-specific: the materials are fabrics. Real-world: measured from physical samples, not synthesised. Spatially varying: reflectance captured per surface point (a texture), not one BRDF per material. Multiple views: more than two distinct viewing directions. Flexible coverage: camera and light poses not confined to a fixed dome, gantry or scanner layout — RoboCloth is the only dataset with all five properties. Main baseline: UBO2014 is the test benchmark of the chart; MERL and UBOFAB19 train the competing decoders.

Reconstruction quality on UBO2014

A controlled protocol: the decoder architecture, renderer and Stage 2 procedure are held fixed across all comparisons, so differences in reconstruction quality are directly attributable to the training data — a decoder trained on RoboCloth, UBOFAB19 or MERL, fitted to 12 held-out UBO2014 materials; PBR is an analytic fit with no learned prior.

Qualitative comparisons

Same protocol as above — the decoder, renderer and Stage 2 fitting are fixed and only the Stage 1 training data changes. Every recovered material is relit in a common scene: the same cloth mesh draped over a bar, with identical UV tiling, lighting and camera for every method, so any visible difference is due solely to the learned material.
